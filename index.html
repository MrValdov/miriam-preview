Leadership insights / 06

You don’t have to be the loudest person in the room.

Miriam reflects on introverted leadership, learning from different styles, and leading through your own strengths.

Read Miriam’s leadership reflection

Lead with your strengths

Have you ever felt out of place as a leader because you’re not the loudest person in the room?

Leadership is not a personality contest. Visibility and assertiveness can shape how people perceive a leader, but perception is not the same as impact.

A different perspective on leadership

As an introverted manager leading a 100-person plant, I worked with a clearly extroverted manager. We had our alignments—and our clashes. We presented ourselves differently, challenged others differently, and interacted with our team in different ways.

At the time, I told myself we were simply two leaders with different styles. Yet I was also trying to validate my own. A training experience he organized for the leadership team changed how I understood effective leadership.

What stayed with me

  • Introversion and extroversion exist on a spectrum.
  • Introversion does not mean avoiding people.
  • Understanding how you recharge helps you understand your own needs.
  • Quietness does not necessarily mean shyness or a lack of confidence.

You don’t need to become an extrovert to lead effectively. Understand your strengths and flex when it matters.
